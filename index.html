Monday · Climate

Climate Change & Agriculture

Productivity, heat, drought and shifting rainfall

Climate adaptation
AFS-1-2026

How has human-induced climate change affected agricultural productivity?

Ortiz-Bobea et al. (2021) estimated that human-induced climate change slowed growth in global agricultural total factor productivity by about 21% since 1961, compared with a no-climate-change scenario. This does not mean agricultural output fell by 21%.

اورتیز-بوبئا و همکاران (۲۰۲۱) برآورد کردند که تغییر اقلیم ناشی از فعالیت انسان، رشد بهره‌وری کل عوامل تولید کشاورزی جهان را از سال ۱۹۶۱ در مقایسه با سناریوی بدون تغییر اقلیم حدود ۲۱ درصد کاهش داده است؛ این عدد به معنای کاهش ۲۱ درصدی تولید نیست.

Read on LinkedIn ↗

Scientific interpretation

The reported 21% concerns the growth trajectory of global agricultural total factor productivity relative to a counterfactual scenario—not a 21% decline in total food output.

Resilience question

Which locally relevant measures can reduce exposure to heat, drought and changing rainfall patterns?

Tuesday · Soil

Healthy Soil, Healthy Crops

The rhizosphere and plant–microbe relationships

Soil biology
AFS-2-2026

Why does the rhizosphere matter for crop nutrition?

The rhizosphere connects roots with bacteria, fungi and other organisms that influence nutrient cycling and uptake. Nutrients present in soil are not necessarily available to plants, and microbial benefits depend on local conditions.

ریزوسفر ناحیه‌ای است که ریشه‌ها را با باکتری‌ها، قارچ‌ها و دیگر جانداران خاکزی پیوند می‌دهد و بر چرخه و جذب عناصر غذایی اثر می‌گذارد. وجود عناصر در خاک الزاماً به معنای قابل‌جذب بودن آن‌ها نیست و اثر میکروب‌ها به شرایط محلی وابسته است.

Read on LinkedIn ↗

Scientific interpretation

Soil nutrient content and plant-available nutrients are not interchangeable. Root-associated organisms can influence nutrient transformations and uptake, but outcomes are context-dependent.

Resilience question

How can soil biological activity be assessed alongside crop nutrition and productivity?

Wednesday · Water

Water-Smart Agriculture

Sensors, satellites, GIS and AI for water decisions

Precision water management
AFS-3-2026

Can digital tools make agricultural water use more responsible?

Sensors, satellites, GIS and AI can guide irrigation and water monitoring. Unreliable data weaken decisions, while higher on-farm irrigation efficiency does not automatically produce net water savings across a river basin.

حسگرها، ماهواره‌ها، GIS و هوش مصنوعی می‌توانند به تصمیم‌گیری آبیاری و پایش آب کمک کنند. داده‌های نامطمئن تصمیم‌ها را تضعیف می‌کنند و افزایش راندمان آبیاری در مزرعه لزوماً به صرفه‌جویی خالص آب در کل حوضه آبریز منجر نمی‌شود.

Read on LinkedIn ↗

Scientific interpretation

Digital tools are only as useful as the data, assumptions and decisions built around them. Field-level efficiency and basin-level water savings are different measures.

Resilience question

Are water withdrawals, return flows and basin conditions being considered—not just irrigation efficiency on individual farms?

Thursday · Regenerative

Organic & Regenerative Agriculture

Practices, outcomes and trade-offs

Outcome-based assessment
AFS-4-2026

How can we tell whether a farming system is actually recovering?

Cover crops, rotations and reduced tillage are practices—not proof of recovery. Progress should be assessed through soil health, yields, water use, biodiversity, emissions and farm economics, including trade-offs.

کشت گیاهان پوششی، تناوب زراعی و کاهش خاک‌ورزی، اقدام و روش هستند نه اثبات قطعی احیای اکوسیستم. پیشرفت باید با شاخص‌هایی مانند سلامت خاک، عملکرد، مصرف آب، تنوع زیستی، انتشار آلاینده‌ها و اقتصاد مزرعه و نیز بده‌بستان‌های میان آن‌ها سنجیده شود.

Read on LinkedIn ↗

Scientific interpretation

A named practice does not establish an environmental or economic outcome by itself. Baselines, indicators, time horizons and trade-offs matter.

Resilience question

Which indicators should be tracked together to avoid judging a system from one practice or one metric?

Friday · Food innovation

Food Security & Vertical Farming

Controlled environments and local leafy greens

Complementary food production
AFS-5-2026

Where can vertical farming contribute—and where are its limits?

Controlled-environment farms can produce local leafy greens using stacked growing space and recirculating water. Energy requirements, costs and crop limitations mean vertical farming complements field agriculture rather than replacing it.

مزارع محیط کنترل‌شده می‌توانند با استفاده از فضای طبقاتی و گردش مجدد آب، سبزی‌های برگی را در نزدیکی بازار تولید کنند. نیاز انرژی، هزینه‌ها و محدودیت نوع محصولات نشان می‌دهد که کشت عمودی مکمل کشاورزی مزرعه‌ای است، نه جایگزین آن.

Read on LinkedIn ↗

Scientific interpretation

Vertical farming has a defined role in selected crops and contexts. Its contribution to food security must be evaluated alongside energy demand, operating costs and the wider food system.

Resilience question

Which crops and locations justify controlled-environment production when compared with field-grown alternatives?

The week’s resilience framework

From climate pressure to resilient food systems

Resilience is not delivered by a single technology or practice. It emerges when interacting parts of the food system are managed together and outcomes are measured at the appropriate scale.

01 · AdaptRespond to climate risks
02 · RegenerateSupport living soils
03 · ManageUse water responsibly
04 · MeasureTrack outcomes + trade-offs
05 · InnovateApply suitable technologies

Five lessons for resilient agriculture

Principles connecting this week’s scientific themes.

01 · Climate contextAssess local exposure to heat, drought and rainfall shifts.
02 · Living soilDistinguish nutrient presence from plant availability.
03 · Water scaleSeparate field efficiency from basin-wide savings.
04 · Real outcomesMeasure multiple indicators and trade-offs.
05 · Fit-for-purpose techUse innovation where its benefits justify its costs.
